This document was first written on Saturday morning as Operation Epic Fury began. It is now Monday. The situation has changed more in 60 hours than most analysts predicted in 60 days.

Ayatollah Ali Khamenei — who has ruled Iran since 1989 — is confirmed dead. More than 40 senior officials and commanders were killed in the opening strikes, including Defence Minister Aziz Nasirzadeh, IRGC commander Mohammad Pakpour, Chief of Staff Abdolrahim Mousavi, and former president Mahmoud Ahmadinejad. An Interim Leadership Council has formed under Article 111 of Iran's constitution. Ahmad Vahidi has been named new IRGC commander-in-chief. CIA assessments predict an IRGC hardliner will ultimately claim the Supreme Leader role.

Meanwhile: Hezbollah has fired missiles at northern Israel for the first time since the November 2024 ceasefire. Israel has responded with strikes on Beirut. Iran struck Saudi Arabia's Aramco facility at Ras Tanura — one of the world's largest oil terminals. Three US service members are dead. Over 14,000 flights have been cancelled. The UAE has closed schools through Wednesday. Ben Gurion airport remains shut. Three US Air Force F-15Es were shot down over Kuwait in a friendly fire incident; pilots bailed safely.

And this morning — the part that changed everything for Britain — an Iranian Shahed-136 drone struck the runway at RAF Akrotiri in Cyprus at 00:03 local time. British sovereign territory in Europe has been hit for the first time. Two further drones were intercepted. The government said the UK is "not at war." Defence analysts called it a wake-up call for a base with "no adequate air defence."

Essay I · Updated Day 3

Britain: From Bystander to Target in 60 Hours

On Saturday morning the question was hypothetical: could Britain be drawn in? By Monday it has a specific answer. An Iranian drone has struck British territory. Starmer has authorised US use of British bases for offensive operations. RAF jets are actively shooting down Iranian drones. Trump publicly rebuked the PM for not moving faster — "very disappointed in Keir," he told The Daily Telegraph, saying it "took far too long."

Saturday: "Britain Is Not At War"

Starmer confirmed UK forces were engaged in defensive operations and declined to authorise offensive use of UK bases. The Attorney General had questioned the legality of the original strikes. Labour backbench opposition was near-universal. The Foreign Affairs Committee chair said on the BBC: "I don't think there's a legal basis for this action."

Sunday Night: The Reversal

Under pressure from Gulf leaders who had been directly struck by Iranian missiles, Starmer reversed and authorised the US to use British bases for the "specific and limited defensive purpose" of targeting Iranian missile depots and launchers. He published a summary of the government's legal position — collective self-defence. He stressed British forces would not participate in offensive strikes on Iran itself. An RAF Typhoon from Qatar, operating with the joint UK-Qatar 12 Squadron, shot down an Iranian drone heading for Qatari territory.

Monday: First Drone Hits British Soil

At 00:03 Cyprus local time, a Shahed-136 — identical to the drones Russia has been using in Ukraine — hit the runway at RAF Akrotiri. Minor material damage, no casualties. Two further drones intercepted. Sirens sounded again during Monday morning. Non-essential personnel and families are being evacuated to alternative accommodation. Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper confirmed the strike hit the airport runway. The UK's stated terror threat level is under review by the Defence Secretary.

"Starmer made the right call in not participating in the initial strikes. He made the wrong call in believing that made them immune to Iranian retaliation. There should have been preparation."

— UK Defence Journal, 2 March 2026

The Structural Logic

What the 60-hour arc reveals is the trap Britain has been in since this conflict became visible. Britain's security is architecturally inseparable from the US — intelligence systems, weapons platforms, communications infrastructure, the RAF's own operational doctrine. When the US goes to war in a region where British assets are physically present, British neutrality is not really an option. Iran knows this. Iran proved it knows this.

The WWII parallel that seemed dramatic last week is now more structurally grounded. Not because bombs are falling on London — they aren't — but because Britain is militarily involved in a conflict Parliament has not voted for, against an enemy actively targeting British territory, with an American ally that is explicitly transactional about its expectations, and with armed forces at a readiness level widely described as dangerously inadequate. The absence of Sky Sabre air defence systems at Akrotiri before the war started — despite weeks of visible US military buildup — is an exhibit in that case.

⚠ Parliament Reconvenes Wednesday

Starmer has authorised offensive use of British bases without a parliamentary vote. Post-Iraq, it is convention (not law) that prime ministers seek approval for major military operations. British sovereign territory has now been struck in retaliation. Parliament returns from recess Wednesday. This will be the first real democratic reckoning. The government's legal position — "collective self-defence" — is published. Whether Parliament accepts it is another question.

Updated Probability Assessment

We previously put a WWII-scale emergency for Britain at 5–10%. We are revising this to 10–18%. British sovereign territory has been struck. The base is now being used for offensive operations. The legal justification of collective self-defence expands rather than limits further obligations as the conflict widens. Hezbollah's re-entry puts Lebanon active, which puts the eastern Mediterranean active, which makes RAF Akrotiri a potential sustained front. The question is no longer whether Britain is involved — it demonstrably is — but whether Parliament endorses and extends it, and whether the Russian opportunism scenario provides the final structural catalyst.

✓ The Limiting Factors

Starmer has drawn firm lines: no British troops in offensive strikes, no use of UK bases against political or economic targets. Iran's FM told Oman he remains "open to de-escalation." Trump says Iranian officials want to talk. The Omani framework still technically exists. Britain's Trident deterrent makes a direct existential attack on the UK homeland essentially irrational for any actor. The EU is standing "collectively" with Cyprus.

Essay II · Updated Day 3

Nuclear Risk: The Succession Crisis Changes the Calculus

On Saturday, the nuclear threat analysis was primarily structural — expired New START, the Doomsday Clock at 89 seconds, missing enriched uranium. By Monday there is a new and specific acute factor: Iran is now a nuclear-adjacent state with no functioning head of government, an active succession crisis, and some IRGC units explicitly confirmed as operating without central command.

The Institute for Science and International Security had assessed, as of mid-February, that the probability of Iran building nuclear weapons was "around 50%, or even a little lower." That assessment assumed a functioning Iranian state making rational strategic decisions. That assumption no longer holds with certainty.

The Enriched Uranium Problem

After the June 2025 strikes, the Bulletin confirmed 400+ kg of uranium enriched to 60% — enough for several nuclear weapons even without further enrichment — was unaccounted for. No IAEA inspector has accessed Iranian nuclear sites since June 2025. CENTCOM stated today that UN nuclear watchdog chief Grossi found "no evidence nuclear facilities were hit" in this operation. The Bushehr area was struck; whether the reactor was damaged is unclear.

The material exists. Its location is not publicly confirmed. The state that owned it has lost its head of government, its defence minister, its chief of staff, and dozens of IRGC commanders. Some units are explicitly confirmed as operating on autonomous pre-programmed instructions. This is the most acute non-proliferation scenario in the post-Cold War era.

Three Nuclear Pathways

Pathway 1 — Autonomous IRGC faction moves enriched material. The most novel risk since Saturday. If an IRGC faction operating on pre-programmed doctrine moves enriched material to a previously unknown facility without central political authority, the result is a de facto nuclear programme below diplomatic radar. Israel's preventive doctrine has modelled this exact scenario for decades.

Pathway 2 — IRGC hardliner restoration with breakout doctrine. CIA assessments suggest the IRGC will push for a hardliner Supreme Leader. A hardliner who concludes that Iran's fundamental error was not having a nuclear deterrent in February 2026 will face an enormous internal constituency pressing for rapid acquisition. The North Korea parallel is historically persuasive: they built a bomb and are not being regime-changed. Iraq did not and was.

Pathway 3 — Superpower miscalculation cascade. The arms-control vacuum created by New START's expiry means no verification mechanisms exist to distinguish accidental from deliberate escalation if US forces kill Russian advisors embedded with Iranian units, or if a cyber event is misread as a weapons launch.

"Taking out Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei is not the same as regime change. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps is the regime."

— Council on Foreign Relations, 1 March 2026

Honest Probability

We estimate nuclear use by any actor in the next 12 months at 3–6%, up from 2–5%. The increase comes from the succession crisis and autonomous IRGC scenario, not from any direct move toward weapons use by a coherent Iranian state. A 3–6% annual probability sounds small — but compounded over a decade it approaches 30–50%, which is the range that causes historians to later ask "how did no one act?" The mitigating factors are real: MAD constrains superpowers, Iran has never used chemical weapons it possessed, the IRGC understands nuclear use invites annihilation. But the IRGC also just lost its central command structure.

📌 Single Most Actionable Fact

The IAEA needs emergency access to Iranian nuclear sites — now, under whatever authority is currently governing Iran, with whatever guarantees can be arranged. The Interim Council has an interest in demonstrating the Islamic Republic remains a functional state. This is a potential diplomatic lever the Omani back-channel should exploit immediately. The enriched uranium question cannot wait weeks for a succession to resolve.
